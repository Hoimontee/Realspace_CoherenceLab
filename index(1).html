How we work

Method

A standard pipeline from raw detector output to a structural answer you can defend — adapted to your instrument, your source, and your question.

01

Data reduction

2D detector frames or single-channel scans, calibrated, masked, and integrated to 1D intensity profiles or total scattering structure functions S(Q). Lab, synchrotron, and time-of-flight neutron sources.

02

Structural refinement

Rietveld and Le Bail refinement against your model — phase fractions, lattice parameters, atomic positions, thermal parameters, preferred orientation, microstrain and crystallite size.

03

Total scattering / PDF

Where average-structure models fall short — nanoparticles, disordered frameworks, local distortions — we extract G(r) and fit real-space structural models over a chosen r-range.

04

Reporting

Fits, uncertainties, and a written interpretation your team — and your reviewers — can act on. Refinement files and scripts included, not just a PDF report.

Where this matters

Applications

Scattering analysis is generic; the interpretation isn't. A sample of the problems we're usually called in for.

Batteries

Cathode & anode electrodes

Cathode and anode materials often look ordered on average and disordered locally. PDF resolves cation mixing, Jahn–Teller distortion, and short-range order that Bragg peaks alone miss.

Pharma

Solid-form characterization

Polymorph identification, amorphous content quantification, and crystallinity tracking through formulation, processing, and stability studies.

Catalysis

Nanoparticles & supported catalysts

Sub-10 nm particles give broadened, weak Bragg patterns. Total scattering recovers coordination environments and particle-scale structure conventional refinement can't reach.

Materials

Glasses & amorphous solids

No Bragg peaks at all. PDF is often the only quantitative route to short- and medium-range order in disordered materials.
